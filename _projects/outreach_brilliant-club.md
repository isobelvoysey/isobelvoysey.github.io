I have worked with [The Brilliant Club](https://thebrilliantclub.org/) for five years, mainly as a PhD tutor on [The Scholars Programme](https://thebrilliantclub.org/the-scholars-programme/). The Scholars Programme is an eight-week course of university-style teaching, designed and delivered by PhD tutors on topics linked to their research, with the aim to develop pupils’ knowledge, skills, and confidence needed to secure a place at a competitive university.

For my course, “Social robots: Boring machines or best mates”, I taught the pupils about some key concepts from human-robot interaction, including perceived agency, anthropomorphism, and embodiment. In order to develop the course, I used backwards planning, starting with what I wanted the pupils to understand and be able to do at the end of the course and working backwards to plan the tutorial activities and homework tasks. As part of my role as their tutor, I provided summative and formative feedback to the pupils. You can find the handbook given to pupils here.

I’ve had the privilege of working with some amazing pupils during my time on the programme and have loved seeing the pupils grow in confidence in their abilities. A particular highlight was when one of my pupils, Daisy, had her final assignment accepted for publication in [The Scholar](https://thebrilliantclub.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/The-Scholar-20.pdf) – a journal published by the Brilliant Club showcasing outstanding work from pupils on The Scholars Programme.

Over my time working with The Brilliant Club, my skills as a tutor and commitment to the organisation’s mission mean I’ve been invited to work on other projects, including [The Turing Summer Experience](https://www.turing.ac.uk/opportunities-turing/turing-summer-experience), a data science and AI summer school run by the Alan Turing Institute, and a version of The Scholars Programme for care-experienced students.

More recently I’ve been invited to work on [The Computer Science Scholars Programme](https://thebrilliantclub.org/news/new-partnership-the-hg-foundation/), which aims to support female and non-binary sixth form students from disadvantaged backgrounds to pursue computer science degrees. For this I’ve developed a new course on recommender systems, introducing pupils to the basics of machine learning, including vector embeddings and neural networks for predictions. Throughout the course, I’m applying [Critically Conscious Computing](https://criticallyconsciouscomputing.org) methods to foster critical reflection on the design and use of recommendation algorithms.
